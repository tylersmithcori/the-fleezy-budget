                Learn money basics

                Short, plain-language lessons built from trusted sources — the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), FDIC Money Smart, Khan Academy, NerdWallet, and Bank of America’s Better Money Habits (bank-provided education). General education only, not personal financial advice.

                LESSON 01Start with what actually comes in›

                A budget only works off real numbers. Write down your take-home pay — what actually lands in your account — not the salary number before tax.

                In this tool, that is your Primary income. Add any second income as Secondary so you can see which money your bills depend on.

                Source: CFPB — Budgeting tools
                LESSON 02Give every dollar a job›

                Before the month starts, decide where the money goes: bills first, then needs, then wants, then savings. When every dollar has a job, money stops “disappearing.”

                The number at the top of My budget — Left after bills & spending — is the dollars that do not have a job yet.

                Source: NerdWallet — How to budget
                LESSON 03The 50 / 30 / 20 guide›

                A simple guide, not a rule: about 50% of take-home for needs (rent, bills, groceries), 30% for wants, and 20% for savings and extra debt payments.

                If your numbers do not fit yet, that is normal — especially with high rent. Use it as a direction to move toward, not a grade.

                Source: NerdWallet — 50/30/20 budget
                LESSON 04Pay yourself first›

                Move a set amount to savings as soon as you get paid — before spending starts. Even a small amount counts. Waiting to save “whatever is left” usually means nothing is left.

                In the Income box, pick a set amount or a percent of what you make — it comes off the top before bills and spending.

                Sources: Khan Academy — Personal finance · Better Money Habits
                LESSON 05Due days are the whole game›

                Most late fees do not happen because there was no money — they happen because the due day and payday did not line up. Knowing every bill’s due day lets you see a tight week before it hits.

                That is why bills in this tool are set once in Monthly bills and repeat: the due day is always in front of you.

                Source: CFPB — Budgeting
                LESSON 06Build a small emergency cushion›

                Start with a small first goal — $500, then $1,000 — in a separate savings account at your own bank. It turns a flat tire or a cut shift from a crisis into an inconvenience, and keeps it off a credit card.

                Sources: FDIC Money Smart · Better Money Habits
                LESSON 07Two ways to pay off debt›

                Snowball: pay the smallest balance first for quick wins, minimums on the rest. Avalanche: pay the highest interest rate first — costs less in total. Both work. The best one is the one you will stick with.

                Always pay at least the minimum on every debt so due days do not hurt your credit.

                Source: CFPB — Debt and budgeting resources
                LESSON 08The 10-minute weekly check-in›

                Once a week, for ten minutes: check your bank balance, look at what is due before your next payday, and add any spending you missed. Small, regular check-ins beat one big stressful catch-up.

                Source: FDIC Money Smart
